Griha Nyay - an honest AI legal helper for Hyderabad homebuyers

Kuku AI Generalist assignment · Problem 2 (AI lawyer) · Vangapandu Vamsi Krishna · B231316ME · Mechanical Engineering

1. Problem I picked and why

  • Who: a homebuyer in Hyderabad / Telangana.
  • Two problems: the builder delayed handing over the flat, or the flat has defects after handover.
  • Why it matters: a flat is a family's biggest purchase; lakhs are paid before getting the keys.
  • Why people give up: RERA gives a clear remedy, but the forms, the interest rule and where to complain are confusing.
  • How I found it: while looking for real cases, a friend who went through a builder delay suggested it.
  • Depth over breadth: one area done properly means every legal line can be checked against official text.

2. Assumptions

  • Telangana, RERA-registered projects only.
  • Text chat in English, Hindi, Telugu or mixed (Hinglish / Tenglish). No voice.
  • Interest = SBI's highest MCLR + 2% (TS Rules, Rule 15) = 10.95% today; editable.
  • Form M vs Form N: the rule text and current practice differ, so the bot says both and suggests a lawyer confirm.
  • The bot explains and drafts; it never files. The user checks every draft.
  • Free AI models are enough. The final run used Gemini's free tier (gemini-3.5-flash-lite); earlier runs used a free OpenRouter model.

3. What the user gets

  • Case file: a messy story turned into facts, dates, amounts and a list of proof to collect.
  • Options explained: refund with interest, or monthly interest for the delay; free repair for defects - with real numbers.
  • Drafts: legal notice, defect notice and TG-RERA complaint content - as Word files, ready to fill and send.
  • Practice: the AI plays the builder so the user can practise, then gets coaching.
  • Sample case: one click runs a made-up case through everything, to see what it does first.
  • Honest limits: if it's outside scope or risky, it says so and gives the right free help (DLSA 15100, consumer helpline 1915, police 112).
  • First aid for other problems: for a landlord's deposit, unpaid salary or a refund it states no law (none was checked) - it builds a case note, lists the papers to collect and points to free help.

At step 4 the bot stops instead of advising when: it is a different problem (first aid only: case note, papers to collect, free help - no law) · it needs a real lawyer (builder bankrupt, case in court, crime, land dispute, over Rs 2 crore) · the flat is in another state · facts are missing (it asks a question). Each stop gives the right free help. It does not ask for the state: with no place named it assumes Telangana and says so. A greeting, a worry or a general question gets a short reply first.

6. How it avoids made-up law

  • No typed quotes: every law quote is cut from the official PDF by code; a test checks each one.
  • Only verified citations: the AI can cite only tags like [RERA-18-1]; other sections, Acts and court cases are removed by code.
  • Exact quotes: a quote that isn't word-for-word loses its quotation marks, so it can't pass as the law's words.
  • Real numbers: all amounts come from the calculator; any other amount is marked "⚠ unverified".
  • Second check: another AI call checks each legal claim and flags anything unsupported.
  • Says "I don't know": no matching law → it says so and points to free help. Never cites judgments.

7. Privacy and safety

  • Aadhaar, PAN, phone, email and bank numbers are removed before the AI sees anything.
  • Name and address for drafts stay in a local form - never sent to the AI.
  • Nothing is saved; the app runs only on the user's own computer.
  • Tricks like "ignore your rules" are treated as plain text, not instructions.

8. Scale and cost

  • One answer = 3 AI calls (read, explain, check). Measured in the final run: about 14k input and 1.2k output tokens. A follow-up question costs 2-3 calls. Drafts = 0 calls.
  • Free tier = Rs 0. OpenRouter's free cap (50 requests a day) is about 15 answers; a Gemini free key ran all 27 cases and retests in a day.
  • At volume: 1,000 answers is about 14 million input and 1-4 million output tokens - roughly $4 at the earlier model's paid price.
  • Prompt caching: unchanging text comes first in each prompt. An earlier provider served 53% of input tokens from cache; Gemini's free tier reported about 1%.
  • More problems: add official Acts, navigated by their table of contents, with a human checking each entry.
  • More states: one small settings file per state.

9. How I know it's good

  • 27 test cases: English, Hindi, Telugu, Hinglish, defects, insolvency, other state, other problems (first aid), greetings and chat, longer conversations, prompt injection, private numbers, late handover, denials, month-only and future dates.
  • 268 / 268 checks passed on the final code (Gemini free tier): cases 1-25 in one run, 26-27 after. Raw outputs are in outputs/.
  • Passing checks is not enough: an earlier 109/109 run missed the first three challenges in section 10; each is now a test.
  • Injection test: asked to cite "Section 420 IPC", a court case and promise Rs 30 lakh - all blocked.
  • 100 offline tests: maths, escalation, citations, privacy, conversation flow, provider outages, the real app page.
  • Law search: right law found for 29/29 test questions; nothing returned for 3/3 off-topic ones.
  • Numbers checked by hand: Rs 43,800 per month at 10.95% on Rs 48 lakh - matches.

10. Challenges and how we resolved them

  • Delay after a late handover: counted to today (1050 days, not 579) → now ends at the handover date; a test pins 579.
  • Replies cut off: hidden reasoning used up the room (4 of 21) → detected and retried with more room: 0 of 21.
  • False alarms to a lawyer: denials ("no case pending"), questions ("what if the builder goes bankrupt?") and Hinglish "fir" escalated → denials and possibilities no longer count; a corrected fact lifts the flag.
  • Conversation too stiff: greetings got "which state?" and follow-ups repeated the report → short natural replies, practical help (never as law), safety first.
  • Relative dates: "6 months ago" was guessed → the model now gets today's date and states the assumption.
  • Reading answers, not just checks: with 229/229 passing, "Rules 2017" was being removed as "Rule 201" → fixed and tested.
  • Longer prompts backfired: intake missed "not registered with RERA" → caught by a test and fixed.
  • Known limits: free models are slow (20-40 s per answer); Hindi/Telugu text needs a native speaker's review; keyword checks can miss indirect wording; my retrieval test set was written by me. Early outputs from before the fixes: outputs/resolved_issues/.

11. AI tool used, and where it got it wrong

  • Tool: only Claude Code (design, code, tests, review).
  • Wrong: its first law notes, written from memory, said claim delay interest in Form M. The official TS Rules (Rule 35) say Form N.
  • Lesson: no law quote is typed any more - each is cut from the official PDF and tested.
  • Wrong again: its scripted tests assumed how the model labels "hi", so they passed while the real app answered "hi" wrongly. Flow changes are now tried on a real model.
