**Your case in short**
From the dates you gave, the delay is 959 days (~31.5 months) past the promised possession date.

**Where you stand**
The law supports buyers when a builder fails to give possession on time. Under [RERA-18-1] "If the promoter fails to complete or is unable to give possession of an apartment", you have the right to seek a remedy. Furthermore, [RERA-19-4] states that "The allottee shall be entitled to claim the refund of amount paid along with interest".

**Your two choices**
(a) Withdraw from the project and get your money back with interest and compensation. Based on your payment of Rs 40,00,000, the refund interest so far is unknown because we need the exact payment dates, and the refund total so far is unknown. 
(b) Stay in the project and get interest for every month of delay until handover. By my calculation, the monthly interest is Rs 36,500, and the total interest accrued so far is Rs 11,50,800. 
Since your goal is to get your money back, choice (a) fits your goal. 

**What to do next**
1. Collect proof: gather your payment receipts, bank statements, and your agreement for sale.
2. Check the project's registration on the TG-RERA website as explained in [FAQ-3].
3. Send a dated written notice to the builder and keep proof of delivery.
4. File a complaint. Rule entries and their practice notes describe filing complaints with the Authority in Form 'M' and claims for interest and compensation with the adjudicating officer in Form 'N' [TS-R34-1] [TS-R35-1].

**What I'm not sure about**
I am not sure about the exact payment dates, the project's RERA registration status, whether the project has prior approvals, or the exact terms in your agreement for sale. The final interest rate, period, and compensation are decided entirely by the authority.

**When to get a real lawyer**
Consult a lawyer or legal aid [LSA-12] to review your agreement, draft your legal notice, and file your complaint correctly.
